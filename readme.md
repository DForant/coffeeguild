# The Coffee Guild - Brand Identity & Website Brief

## Client Questionaire

1. Why do you want to have a new website, or have your current site redesigned?

Honestly, right now we don't have much of an online presence. We're a brick-and-mortar shop that's been relying on foot traffic and word-of-mouth. But Jacksonville is growing, and I know we're missing out on reaching a wider audience and building stronger connections with our regulars. A professional website and brand identity will help us establish credibility, showcase what makes us special, and ultimately drive more business both online and in-store. 


1. What will happen if you don’t have a new website, or have your current site redesigned?

If we don't get this done, I worry we'll get left behind. Other coffee shops in the area are stepping up their online game, and we risk appearing outdated and less accessible. We'll continue to rely solely on local foot traffic, limiting our growth potential and our ability to connect with potential customers who might be searching for a great coffee experience online. We'll miss out on opportunities for online orders, catering inquiries, and building a loyal online community.

3. Please describe your organization in a few sentences.

The Coffee Guild is more than just a coffee shop; it's a community hub built on a passion for exceptional coffee and genuine connection. We meticulously source high-quality beans, expertly craft each drink, and strive to create a warm and inviting atmosphere where everyone feels welcome. We're dedicated to the art and craft of coffee, hence the name "The Coffee Guild."

4. What is there about you and your background that sets you apart for a special (niche) group of potential customers?

Before opening The Coffee Guild, I spent over a decade traveling the world, working on coffee farms and learning directly from growers. I have a deep understanding of the entire coffee process, from bean to cup, and a real appreciation for the ethical and sustainable practices involved. This firsthand experience allows me to select truly unique and exceptional beans that you won't find just anywhere. I want to share this knowledge and passion with coffee enthusiasts who appreciate quality and the story behind their brew.

5. What problems do your prospects have that your business solves?

Our prospects are often tired of mediocre coffee and impersonal service. They're looking for a place where they can get a truly excellent cup of coffee, learn something new about it if they're interested, and feel like they're part of a community. We solve the problem of a transactional coffee experience by offering quality, knowledge, and a welcoming atmosphere.

6. How can your particular work background help prospects, compared to others in your industry? What’s special about your work experience?

My direct experience working on coffee farms gives me an insider's perspective that most coffee shop owners don't have. I can speak authentically about the origin of our beans, the farmers who grew them, and the sustainable practices we support. This allows me to offer a level of transparency and knowledge that builds trust and resonates with customers who care about where their coffee comes from. It's not just about selling coffee; it's about sharing a story and a passion.

7. Why do you believe site visitors should do business with you rather than with a competitor?

We offer a unique combination of exceptional coffee quality, in-depth knowledge, and a genuine sense of community. We're not just another coffee shop; we're a place where you can taste the difference that passion and expertise make. Our commitment to sourcing ethically and sustainably also sets us apart. We want our customers to feel good about the coffee they're drinking and the values we uphold.

8. Do you have a slogan or tagline that clearly describes what you offer in terms of benefits or features?

We're still working on solidifying this, but something along the lines of: "The Coffee Guild: Crafted Coffee, Cultivated Community." or perhaps "Savor the Source. Share the Experience." I'm open to your creative suggestions here!

9. Please describe your potential customers. Pay special attention to their income, interests, gender, age, even type of computer they use.

Our ideal customers are likely to be:

Age: 25-55 years old, with a slight skew towards the 30-45 age range.
Income: Middle to upper-middle class, with disposable income to spend on quality food and beverages.
Interests: They appreciate quality, craftsmanship, and authenticity. They might be interested in food and drink culture, sustainability, travel, and supporting local businesses. They likely value experiences over just products.
Gender: Fairly even split between male and female.
Tech Savvy: Comfortable using computers, smartphones, and social media. They likely use a mix of desktop and mobile devices to browse the internet. They might be active on platforms like Instagram and local community groups.

11. Have you researched your online competition so you have an idea of what you do and don’t want on your site?

I've done some initial browsing. I see a lot of coffee shops with basic websites that just list their menu and hours. Some have more visually appealing sites with nice photography, but often lack depth in terms of their story or the coffee itself. I don't want our site to just be a digital menu. I want it to be an extension of our shop's experience – informative, engaging, and reflective of our passion.

16. What do you NOT want on your site in terms of text, content, etc.?

I definitely want to avoid generic, overly corporate language. We're a small, passionate business, and our website should reflect that authenticity. I also don't want overly cluttered pages or anything that feels difficult to navigate. We should avoid jargon that our average customer won't understand unless it's clearly explained. And absolutely no stock photos of generic coffee! We need to showcase our unique offerings and atmosphere.

17. Are you planning to do online sales or eCommerce? If so describe the type of product and what the process will be for the customer.

Initially, we're not planning for full-scale e-commerce with shipping. However, I would like to explore the possibility of online ordering for local pickup. This would allow customers to order their favorite drinks and pastries ahead of time and swing by to grab them. The process would ideally be: customer browses a simple online menu, selects their items, customizes options (milk, sugar, etc.), pays securely online, and then receives a notification when their order is ready for pickup. This would be a great convenience for our regulars and could attract new customers looking for a quick and easy way to get their coffee.

18. If you were using a search engine, what words or phrases would you use to find your site? Which of these words or phrases is most important? Second? Third?

Most Important: "coffee shop Jacksonville"
Second: "best coffee Jacksonville"
Third: "local coffee Jacksonville"
Other potential phrases: "specialty coffee Jacksonville," "organic coffee Jacksonville," "coffee roasters Jacksonville" (even though we don't roast in-house, we emphasize our sourcing), "coffee and community Jacksonville."
19. Other than what search engines will produce, what methods do you have in mind to spread the word about your website?

We'll definitely promote the website on our social media channels (Instagram, Facebook). We'll also include the website address on our in-store signage, menus, and loyalty cards. Word-of-mouth from our satisfied customers will also be key. I'm open to other suggestions you might have!

20. How do you plan to encourage repeat visitors and referrals?

We aim to provide valuable content on the website, such as blog posts about our coffee origins, brewing tips, and upcoming events. We'll also use social media to engage with our audience and build a community. In-store, we have a loyalty program, and we can promote online engagement through exclusive offers for website visitors or social media followers. Positive customer experiences both online and in-store will be the best driver for referrals.
